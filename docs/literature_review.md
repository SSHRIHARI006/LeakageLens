# Literature review: data leakage and performance inflation

## Method and scope

This review was prepared from all 20 PDFs in the professor-supplied archive. Two pairs are different versions of the same work: Rosenblatt et al. (2023 preprint / 2024 publication) and Briere et al. (2025 preprint / published version). They are inventoried separately below but are cited once in the synthesis. Claims are limited to what the supplied papers report. The proposed application is for tabular ML; image, graph, spatial, and time-series papers are used only for their general evaluation lessons, not as evidence that LeakageLens can diagnose those modalities.

## Synthesis

The collection consistently frames leakage as an evaluation-validity problem: information available during development makes held-out performance look better than the intended inference setting permits. The strongest direct support for this project is for (1) duplication and dependent entities crossing splits, (2) transformations or feature selection fitted outside a training fold, (3) time, campaign, or spatial structure ignored by random splits, (4) target/proxy or circular features, and (5) augmentation/resampling applied before splitting. Multiple empirical studies show that correcting a split or preprocessing order can substantially change scores; they do not justify a universal expected drop.

LeakageLens therefore reports *evidence and risk*, not proof or a calibrated probability. Exact train/test row overlap is deterministic for a given split; group and temporal overlaps are deterministic once their semantics are supplied; feature-target strength, name cues, and near-duplicate similarity are heuristic. A safer re-evaluation is an alternative protocol aligned with user-declared deployment assumptions, not automatically the true performance.

## Boundaries adopted by LeakageLens

- **Directly derived from literature:** audit for exact split overlap; group and temporal split alternatives when their columns/task semantics are declared; train-only fitting recommendation; explicit report of original versus safer evaluation; warnings for pre-split resampling/augmentation patterns.
- **Adapted from literature:** tabular row hashes and practical near-duplicate blocking; single-feature models, association measures, and a small configurable permutation control; simple code-pattern checks.
- **Project extensions:** an interpretable multi-signal 0-100 risk score, unified finding schema, configurable thresholds, Streamlit/FastAPI packaging, and synthetic demos.
- **Engineering choices:** scikit-learn `Pipeline`/`ColumnTransformer` inspection, pandas ingestion, JSON/YAML configuration, bounded computation, and HTML/Markdown reports.

## Limitations of this review

The supplied set is heterogeneous and several works are preprints or domain-specific case studies. Reported performance changes are retained as case-study evidence only; LeakageLens will calculate its own values on each run and will not transplant these numbers to user data. Feature legitimacy, groups, time direction, and intended deployment remain domain decisions that automation can flag but cannot settle.
